Scientific Accuracy & Visual Scaling

In real astronomical space, the Solar System is overwhelmingly vast compared to the sizes of the planets. If modeled at a strict 1:1 linear scale where Earth is 1 pixel wide, the Sun would be 109 pixels wide, and Neptune would orbit more than 350,000 pixels (over 90 screens) away.

What is Artistically Approximated:

  • Compressed Orbital Radii: Planetary distances from the Sun use a gently compressed non-linear spacing so all 8 planets and their orbital rings can be viewed simultaneously.
  • Enlarged Planetary Radii: Planets (and Earth's Moon) are magnified relative to the Sun so you can inspect surface textures, cloud bands, axial tilts, and ring systems in 3D.

What is Scientifically Preserved:

  • Exact Keplerian Orbital Period Ratios: Mercury completes ~4.15 orbits and Jupiter completes ~0.084 orbits for every 1 Earth orbit ($365.256\text{ days}$).
  • Axial Tilts & Retrograde Spin: Venus spins clockwise ($177.3^\circ$ tilt), Uranus rolls on its side ($97.8^\circ$ tilt), and Saturn's rings incline at $26.7^\circ$.
  • NASA/JPL Planetary Telemetry: All figures in the inspector panel reflect true physical measurements.
